Prepost 

I dead-lifted barbells on a sunny midmorning in the converted garage, 

then reclined to super-set sit-ups on the indoor-outdoor carpet; 

good thing I’m already on the floor, 

because my right half no longer responds to my will. 

I ran alone in the bike lane on the right side of the four-lane, 

with close cars’ backwash wicking sweat from my shirtless chest  

basted by a squinting sun; a fortnight later, 

in the shadow-free linoleum conditioned-air hospital hall,  

the therapist squats to swing my foot forward, 

her aide grips the gait belt around my belly, 

and my wife trails a wheelchair to reseat me 

after twenty steps. 

I daily biked to juxtaposed towns in weather excepting snow 

(we have no snow), and slung panniers from the rack 

with produce, staples, beverages, or books, as befits the errand; 

now, the arm bike crank pedals my elastically bandaged grip, 

later I’ll read Outer Dark in disabled parking, windows 

fogged and drizzled, after freeway off-ramp rehab, 

while my wife shops. 

I planed rough planks of six-quarter claro walnut, 

ripped flitch bookmarks, joined live edges, splined ash dados, 

hand-sanded to 400 grit, varnished six coats of satin, 

bowed mahogany legs and eight matching chairs; 

 
 
 
 
lately I surveil that dining set from the sofa, 

socks up on the ottoman, as the handyman, Max, my wife hired, 

sets his power drill on the protective table pad, while he lugs his toolbox 

down to add handicap toilet grab bars to the master wing 

I added on last decade, double single-handed, when able. 

I talked at dinner parties, sharpened my point while others spoke, 

interjected at the drop of a word, elbowed for a laugh, 

uppercut for a knock-out quip, feinted when nobody knew better, 

girded my anecdotes, hearsay, conjectures, fabrications, 

a combatant sporting against three pair of friends; 

these days I practice standup apraxia, wait for it; nobody waits,  

the repartee redirects before my errant motor program plants the sound, 

and when, under affirmative action, acquaintances lull  

for my encoding’s spliced syllables to play back at quarter speed,  

they’re distracting screens, rehearsing phrases, whispering peripheries, 

and toggling the kindness timer. 

I wrote plotless fables without morals for silicon castles I carved, 

soon swept away beneath the tide of Moore’s law, 

tattooed the whirligigs with sung poems in full retreat, 

and left fictional legacies in hidden fortifications; 

when my lips failed, so went the written word, I couldn’t hear myself write, 

and I read flat text that spiked the canon dry as academia, 

deaf to the melodies of unvoiced speech;  

a year later, I blend phonemes again, my inner ear emotes faint print, 

four finger keyboard, in lemon juice, sputtered slow prose, no pastime, 

 (Transtomer pen’d a silent six years, mute forever, after stroke at 59, 

  
 
unspoken question if he heard his unwritten music) 

and I write life lines, unspool red threads in a labyrinth of pending time.
