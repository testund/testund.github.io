Midway on a flight to the Midwest abroad a commuter jet, 
sort of a three-quarters scale model of a regular passenger airplane, 
I had to pee. 
I half-squatted stiffly from my seat in the front of the plane, 
and swiveled out into the aisle facing aft, 
while straightening my legs and bending at my waist 
to cradle the crown of my head below the curved gray ceiling, 
where a metallic vibration muffed my ears. 
Twenty-something rows of miniature seats, 
two pairs of two across a tiny aisle, 
filled the narrow tube of the fuselage,  
every seat fully occupied with an oversized person.  
Along the gauntlet of armrest elbows and disjoint knees and shoes, 
I hobbled half sideways, leading with my game leg,  
my good palm sliding along the overhead compartments 
beside my turtled neck, 
my other arm clenched fist-like below my ribs. 
The lady in 6C wrinkled her upper lip, diagonally across the aisle 
a man glanced away. Most members of my motley audience were too lost 
in screens or airborne in dreams to watch my distant moonwalk, 
the exceptions flipped to other than my channel upon approach.  
At the far end of the tightrope, the attendant turned aside, 
and the silhouette of her navy skirt revealed the dull gray gleam 
of a serving cart, seatback to seatback wide, 
guarding the restroom door. 
She signaled me into an exit row up ahead. 
On my left, two laptops open on two tray tables, 
on the right, ear-buds, white shirt, ankle crossed knee, 
spread sheets highlighted blue marker, 
while the window woman whispers “I think he wants to get in.” 
The mannequin remains rigidly posed, 
so I crouch sideward, the toe of his wingtips grazes my thigh, 
the stubby air outlet indents the nape of my neck. 
When the cart passes, I unfold for the bathroom 
and discover behind the door a suitcase-sized niche 
which spans the width of the tapered tail, 
and extends a shoulder’s breadth deep. 

 
 
The miniscule toilet fills in one third of the vault. 
I bend double resting my forehead against the ceiling arc, 
then try arching my back backwards with my hips in limbo, 
but can’t align my aim anywhere near the rim,  
and couldn’t contort enough to flop down and regain my feet. 
The sole alternative anoints 
the begging bowl-sized sink secured on the bulkhead. 
I squirt ample liquid soap and run the water long after 
the suds disappear from the drain grid, 
washed stainless under gray-green light,  
before returning penitent to my place, 
bearing the lash of twice twelve dozen unseen eyes 
on my back.
