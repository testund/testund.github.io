When I was 10 years old, I wasn’t afraid of what was under my bed, 

or in the closet, or in the dark, or graveyards, or mirrors, or dogs, 

or zombies, or vampires, or anything else. 

What I was afraid of was a Monster, who was everywhere,  

invisible, immortal, more powerful than whole universe, 

and who wanted to destroy me. 

Not only was the Monster always watching me, even in my sleep, 

but He could even read my secret thoughts. 

There was no place to hide, any minute He could kill me. 

My parents told me to beg the Monster for my life, 

on my knees, eyes closed, hands folded, just the position 

that you don’t see what’s coming and can’t defend yourself. 

Not that it would make any difference anyway. 

My parents even made me go with them on weekends to a place 

worse than school, harder seats, more uncomfortable clothes, 

a lot more boring, where everybody begged and sang awful songs 

to the Monster. The principal or whoever he was up in front 

yelled at us on and on, after he read from an old-fashion book about 

what the Monster had done to people in the past, floods, fires, 

earthquakes, pillars of salt, that kind of stuff. 

The Monster had a son that He tortured and killed a long time ago. 

Makes sense, I know what my dad used to do to me if I made him mad, 

I don’t want to think about how much he would’ve hurt me if he had all 

power in the universe. I don’t remember what his mom said about 

the Monster killing their son, whether she cried, or kept quiet, 

 
 
 
or whether she said to her son “Wait until your father gets home!” 

My mom told me that the Monster has mercy for some people, 

for my baby sister I never saw because she never made it home 

from the hospital, because she didn’t live long enough to make Him 

mad at her. Otherwise, He’s bound to punish you forever,  

simply for being born. Funny thing about it is, the Monster knows 

everything about everything, including the future, so what’s the point 

of begging Him to have mercy for me anyway. Sort of like having  

to say “I’m sorry” when my dad slaps me for doing something 

that I didn’t think was wrong, just for my own good. I remember 

this one time, after supper, when my father was reading a chapter 

out of the Monster’s storybook, which we always had to do every night, 

when he read something like, “They make me angry, and lo, they put 

the branch to their nose.” I’m not kidding, that’s what it said. 

And I said, maybe they wiggled their fingers in front their nose, 

and I showed my parents what I meant, and my father knocked me 

off the chair, and yelled at me, “Don’t you ever do that again!” 

but I swear on the Monster I wasn’t trying be a smart aleck at all. 

Here’s the thing. Even though I stopped believing in monsters  

a long time ago, I know that it doesn’t mean that the Monster isn’t real. 

But I’ll be damned if I’ll believe in a monster like that.
