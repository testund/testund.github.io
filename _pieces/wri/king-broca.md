(for Lisa) 

Once upon a time, King Broca ruled over the kingdom of Ischemia. 

One day, he had the misfortune to mistake the Sorcerer Apraxis for the Witch Aphasia, 

whereupon he was cursed by Apraxis with extremely slow speech, 

which made him most unhappy. 

First, Broca decreed that all people in the kingdom should talk not faster than he, 

but he quickly grew impatient with that. 

Second, the King commanded that all his remarks be recorded and 

played back at high speed, but he soon found that he missed conversation.  

Thirdly, he summoned all the speech therapists to his palace in order to treat him, 

one by one, and as they failed to find a cure, he ordered their tongues cut out, 

just to keep the remainder motivated. 

After thirty and eight had plied their various drills, prostheses, and spells, 

and had been rendered speechless therapists, 

a slight, raven-haired woman was ushered into King Broca’s chambers. 

Her name was Aura Lea, and she explained that she couldn’t possibly begin 

a course of treatment before getting to know his Majesty  

and would he please recount his earliest childhood memory.

After more than a year of therapy, he had advanced to the topic of elementary school,

As the months and years went by, the King gradually with practice spoke  

at a more normal pace, or perhaps the Sorcerer Apraxis’ spell was wearing off. 

However, like most people born to power and privilege, Broca was so self-absorbed  

he only could talk about himself, so instead of his subjects 

being bored to suppressed tears by his slow speech, 

they were driven to veiled distraction by its content. 

One day, after some years had passed, and Aura Lea’s hair 

was no longer midnight, but streaked with gray dawn, the King  

did what he never had done before, he asked her a question.  He asked, 

‘Well. that. brings. us. up. to. date., what. do. you. think. about. what. I’ve. told. you?” 

Aura Lea bowed her head, and, after a considerable pause, lifted her eyes. She said, 

“Forgive me, King Broca, I’m sorry, I haven’t been listening. Unfortunately, 

a childhood fever left me quite deaf.” 

The End
